Technique 1 · Few-shot prompting

Show, don’t tell

Prompt
Turn these notes into flashcards: Mitochondria produce ATP through cellular respiration. The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
Turn notes into flashcards. Follow the examples. Note: DNA stores genetic information. Card: Q: What stores genetic information? | A: DNA Note: Ribosomes build proteins. Card: Q: What do ribosomes build? | A: Proteins Note: Mitochondria produce ATP through cellular respiration. Note: The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
Output (example)
Sure! Here are some flashcards for you 📚

1. Mitochondria — Known as the “powerhouse of the cell”, mitochondria make ATP through cellular respiration, which includes glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and…

2. What is the role of the membrane? It is a barrier that…
Q: What produces ATP through cellular respiration? | A: Mitochondria Q: What controls what enters and leaves the cell? | A: The cell membrane

Anthropic suggests 3–5 examples for best results. The idea was made famous by the GPT-3 paper, “Language Models are Few-Shot Learners” (2020).

Toggle between 0 and 2 examples. With zero examples the AI guesses the format; with two it copies your pattern exactly. Great for flashcards, citations, tables, and anything you'll paste into another tool.

2026 update

Reasoning models (like Claude with extended thinking) already think before they answer. For them, a short “think it through carefully” usually works better than writing every step out yourself.

The 2022 result is famous because it's so cheap: one sentence, 4× the accuracy. Press → for the modern twist: today's reasoning models do this automatically, so micromanaging the steps can actually hurt. Ask for careful thinking, not a script.

Technique 3 · Structure

Fence off your data

When you paste notes, emails or articles, wrap them so your instructions and your data don’t get mixed up.

Messy

summarise this for me in bm bullet points Chapter 3 covers supply and demand, where the price of a good is determined by… (2 pages later) …also add a quiz at the end and make it short

Structured 
<notes>
  Chapter 3 covers supply and demand…
</notes>

<task>Summarise the notes in 5 bullet points.</task>
<format>Bahasa Melayu, one line each.
Then 3 quiz questions.</format>

Anthropic recommends XML-style tags like <notes> for Claude. Any clear fence works in other chatbots too: ###, """ or headings.

Long pastes are where prompts go wrong: the AI can't tell where your notes end and your instructions begin. Tags fix that. Bonus: you can refer to them — "use only what's inside <notes>".

Stay safe

Connect with care

Trust the source

Install connectors from official publishers, not random links.

Read before you approve

Connected tools can read, write and send. Check what you’re allowing.

Beware hidden instructions

A web page or file can hide text aimed at the AI. That’s called prompt injection.

Keep secrets out

Never paste passwords, IC numbers or bank details into a chat.

Security researchers have flagged prompt-injection and “tool poisoning” risks in MCP setups since 2025.

Hands can do damage too. Prompt injection is the big one: text hidden in a web page or document that tries to give the AI instructions. Good AI apps ask for permission before acting — read those prompts, don't just click Allow.

Why? Tokenizers learn the most common chunks in their training text. English chunks are everywhere, so English packs tighter.

It’s improving. The older GPT-4 tokenizer needed 25 tokens for the same BM sentence. And it varies by model: Anthropic says Claude 4.7+ produces about 30% more tokens for the same text.

Keep writing in BM — it's fine! Just know that very long BM documents cost a little more, so trimming what you paste matters even more. All numbers here were computed with the real tokenizers, not estimated.

Three reasons

Why tokens matter

Money

AI APIs charge per million tokens. On Claude, output costs 5× input.

e.g. Claude Sonnet 5: US$2 in / US$10 out per million tokens.

Memory

The context window is the AI’s working memory. The whole chat has to fit inside it.

Up to 1 million tokens on newer Claude models.

Quality

Longer isn’t smarter. In tests of 18 models, answers got less reliable as inputs grew.

Chroma, “Context Rot” (2025). Facts in the middle of long prompts get missed most (Liu et al., 2023).

On chat plans with usage limits, long threads burn through your allowance faster.

Most students use free or subscription chat apps, so "money" feels abstract — but usage limits aren't. And the quality point surprises people: dumping more into the chat can make answers worse.

For everyone

The token diet: 8 habits

01

New topic, new chat

Old context rides along with every message.

02

Be specific first

Fewer do-overs means fewer turns.

03

Set the length

“5 bullets.” “Under 100 words.”

04

Paste the part, not the PDF

A 500 kB research PDF ≈ 125,000 tokens.

05

Edit, don’t pile on

Edit your prompt instead of adding “no, I meant…”.

06

Crop screenshots

A full-HD screenshot ≈ 2,700 tokens on newer Claude models.

07

Right-size the model

Small, fast models for simple jobs.

08

Summarise & restart

Ask for a summary, start fresh with it.

Numbers come from Anthropic's docs: a 500 kB research paper is about 125,000 tokens; a 1920×1080 image is 2,691 tokens on Claude 4.7 and later. Habit 5 is the most underrated: editing a message replaces the bad turn instead of stacking it.
